Controls Education with Maglev

Timeline
October 2025 – December 2025
Tools
Software C++, Simulink
Hardware Arduino, IR distance sensor, LCD screen, knobs and buttons
Manufacturing SolidWorks, 3D printing

Feedback control is hard to learn from equations alone, and students build intuition faster when they can change a gain and immediately see a system respond. The objective was a magnetic levitation rig that demonstrates PID control interactively. As the team’s lead for controller programming, I first modeled the nonlinear dynamics of the levitating magnet in Simulink and used that model to select gains for levitation both above and below the device. Those gains carried over to the hardware, where I programmed and tuned a PID position controller that reads the magnet’s height from an IR sensor. To make the rig interactive, I programmed an Arduino interface with an LCD screen, knobs, and buttons for live demonstrations, and I iterated the 3D-printed housing in SolidWorks until it constrained the magnet to smooth vertical motion. The tuned controller held the magnet in stable levitation, and modeling first meant that tuning on the hardware started from gains that already worked in simulation rather than from trial and error.
